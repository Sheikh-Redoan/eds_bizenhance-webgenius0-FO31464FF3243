EVERY PATH — SIMPLE RUSSIAN GRAMMAR & PRONUNCIATION GUIDE
Word-by-word companion for the complete song

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
• Read the English lyric first, then the Russian lyric.
• The accent mark (´) above a vowel shows which syllable to stress: доро́га.
• Ё is always stressed in these lyrics, so an extra accent is not needed.
• Every word below has an everyday meaning and a SIMPLE grammar explanation.
• Pronunciation tips point out the sounds most likely to trip up beginners.
• Accent marks are learning aids; ordinary Russian text normally omits them.

QUICK GRAMMAR KEY
• Noun: a person, place, or thing (road, cars, hill).
• Verb: an action or state (stand, run, fly).
• Adjective: describes a noun (long).
• Adverb: describes an action or direction (here, forward, again).
• Preposition: a short linking word (on, to, along, with).
• Nominative: the doer / the thing being described.
• Accusative: the direct object / what the action affects.
• Genitive: often means “of,” or follows expressions such as до (“until”).
• Dative: often follows к (“toward”) and по (“along”).
• Prepositional: often describes location after на or в.
• Instrumental: often appears after с/со (“with”).
• Present tense: happening now or habitually.
• Perfective future: an action viewed as completed in the future.
• A soft sign (ь) has no vowel sound; it softens the preceding consonant.

LYRIC 1 — HERE WE ARE STANDING ON THE HILL
Russian: Мы стои́м здесь, на верши́не холма́.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Мы — “we”; pronoun; subject (nominative), the people standing.
• стои́м — “(we) stand / are standing”; present-tense verb, “we” form.
• здесь — “here”; place adverb, telling us where.
• на — “on / at”; preposition introducing a location.
• верши́не — “summit / top”; noun in the prepositional case after на.
• холма́ — “of the hill”; noun in the genitive case, from холм (“hill”).

PRONUNCIATION
• стои́м: stress the final syllable; do not say both vowels as one.
• верши́не: stress ШИ; ши is pronounced with a hard Ш sound.
• холма́: stress МА; the initial Х is a throaty, breathy Russian sound.

GRAMMAR IDEA: Мы стои́м = “we are standing.” Russian does not need a separate word for “are” here.

LYRIC 2 — THERE YOU GO WALKING TOWARDS THE END
Russian: А ты идёшь вперёд, к са́мому концу́.

WORD-BY-WORD
• А — “and / but / whereas”; connects this idea to the last one.
• ты — “you”; informal singular subject (nominative).
• идёшь — “you go / are walking”; present-tense verb, “you” form.
• вперёд — “forward”; direction adverb.
• к — “toward / to”; preposition taking the dative case.
• са́мому — “very / ultimate”; emphasizes the endpoint; dative form of са́мый.
• концу́ — “end”; dative form of коне́ц after к.

PRONUNCIATION
• идёшь: Ё is stressed; final Ш is pronounced without an added vowel.
• вперёд: the opening ВП sounds are joined closely; Ё is stressed.
• концу́: stress ЦУ; Ц makes a “ts” sound.

GRAMMAR IDEA: к + dative means movement TOWARD something: к концу́ = “toward the end.”

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 3 — WHEN THEY SEE HOW LONG THE ROAD IS
Russian: Когда́ они́ ви́дят, кака́я дли́нная доро́га.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Когда́ — “when”; connecting word introducing a time clause.
• они́ — “they”; subject (nominative).
• ви́дят — “(they) see”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• кака́я — “what a / how”; feminine form matching доро́га.
• дли́нная — “long”; feminine singular adjective describing доро́га.
• доро́га — “road”; feminine singular noun (nominative).

PRONUNCIATION
• когда́: stress ДА; unstressed О is reduced.
• ви́дят: stress ВИ; the Д before Я sounds soft.
• дли́нная: stress ДЛИ; double Н is not pronounced as an extra syllable.
• доро́га: stress РО, NOT the first or last syllable.

GRAMMAR IDEA: кака́я дли́нная доро́га — all three forms agree with a feminine noun.

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 4 — NOW THEY RUN LEFT AND RIGHT
Russian: Тепе́рь они́ бегу́т то вле́во, то впра́во.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Тепе́рь — “now”; time adverb.
• они́ — “they”; subject (nominative).
• бегу́т — “(they) run / are running”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• то — “now / then / sometimes”; part of a repeated contrast: то … то …
• вле́во — “to the left”; direction adverb.
• то — repeats the alternation: “first … then …”
• впра́во — “to the right”; direction adverb.

PRONUNCIATION
• тепе́рь: stress ПЕ; Ь softens the final Р.
• бегу́т: stress ГУТ.
• вле́во: stress ЛЕ; впра́во: stress ПРА.

GRAMMAR IDEA: то вле́во, то впра́во = “first left, then right / back and forth between left and right.”

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 5 — SPRINTING BACK TO THE BEGINNING
Russian: Сно́ва бегу́т к нача́лу.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Сно́ва — “again”; adverb.
• бегу́т — “(they) run”; present-tense verb; the subject “they” is understood.
• к — “toward / to”; preposition taking the dative case.
• нача́лу — “beginning”; dative form of нача́ло.

PRONUNCIATION
• сно́ва: stress СНО; unstressed final А is light.
• нача́лу: stress ЧА; Ч is a soft “ch”-like sound.

GRAMMAR IDEA: к нача́лу = “back toward the beginning.” The word “they” does not need to be repeated.

LYRIC 6 — EVERY PATH CIRCLES BACK TO THE START
Russian: Ка́ждый путь сно́ва приво́дит к нача́лу.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Ка́ждый — “every / each”; masculine singular adjective modifying путь.
• путь — “path”; masculine singular noun, subject (nominative).
• сно́ва — “again”; adverb.
• приво́дит — “leads / brings”; present-tense verb, “it” form.
• к — “toward / to”; preposition taking the dative case.
• нача́лу — “beginning”; dative form of нача́ло.

PRONUNCIATION
• ка́ждый: stress КАЖ; Ж is the hard Russian zh sound, as in “measure” but firmer.
• путь: final Ь softens Т; there is no extra vowel after it.
• приво́дит: stress ВО; avoid giving every syllable equal weight.

GRAMMAR IDEA: путь ends in Ь but is MASCULINE, which is why ка́ждый agrees with it.

LYRIC 7 — COME WITH ME TO JOURNEY FORWARD
Russian: Пойдём со мной, продо́лжим путь вперёд.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Пойдём — “let's go”; literally “we will set off”; a friendly invitation.
• со — “with”; form of с used before words such as мной.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form of я after со.
• продо́лжим — “(we will) continue”; perfective future, “we” form.
• путь — “path / journey”; direct object (accusative; looks like nominative).
• вперёд — “forward”; direction adverb.

PRONUNCIATION
• пойдём: stress Ё; й sounds like a quick English Y glide.
• мной: pronounce МН closely together, not as two syllables.
• продо́лжим: stress ДО; Ж remains a hard consonant here.
• вперёд: stress Ё.

GRAMMAR IDEA: со мной = “with me.” Пойдём and продо́лжим make a natural “let's go and continue” invitation.

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 8 — COME WITH ME TO SEE IT TILL THE END
Russian: Пойдём со мной, дойдём до са́мого конца́.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Пойдём — “let's go”; invitation / future “we” form.
• со — “with”; preposition.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form.
• дойдём — “(we will) reach / make it to”; perfective future, “we” form.
• до — “until / as far as”; preposition taking the genitive case.
• са́мого — “the very”; masculine genitive adjective emphasizing the end.
• конца́ — “end”; genitive form of коне́ц.

PRONUNCIATION
• дойдём: stress Ё; say the Д-O-Й-Д cluster smoothly.
• са́мого: stress СА; in standard Russian the Г in -ого is pronounced like В.
• конца́: stress ЦА; Ц = “ts.”

GRAMMAR IDEA: до конца́ = “all the way to the end.” Notice the contrast: к концу́ (toward the end) vs. до конца́ (as far as the end).

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 9 — COME WITH ME TO FEEL THE ROAD
Russian: Пойдём со мной, почу́вствуем доро́гу.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Пойдём — “let's go”; invitation.
• со — “with”; preposition.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form.
• почу́вствуем — “(we will) feel / experience”; perfective future, “we” form.
• доро́гу — “road”; direct object (accusative), from доро́га.

PRONUNCIATION
• почу́вствуем: stress ЧУ; slow down for the ВСТВ cluster.
• доро́гу: stress РО; the final -А of доро́га becomes -У here.

GRAMMAR IDEA: доро́га (road as subject) → доро́гу (road as the thing felt). That is the accusative case.

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 10 — COME WITH ME TO EXPLORE THE STREETS
Russian: Пойдём со мной, иссле́дуем у́лицы.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Пойдём — “let's go”; invitation.
• со — “with”; preposition.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form.
• иссле́дуем — “(we will) explore”; verb in the “we” form.
• у́лицы — “streets”; accusative plural noun; the thing explored.

PRONUNCIATION
• иссле́дуем: stress СЛЕ; the two written С letters belong together.
• у́лицы: stress У; Ц is “ts,” and Ы is a Russian back vowel.

GRAMMAR IDEA: у́лицы is plural here; the word is the direct object of иссле́дуем.

LYRIC 11 — COME WITH ME TO JOURNEY ON
Russian: Пойдём со мной, продо́лжим путь.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Пойдём — “let's go”; invitation.
• со — “with”; preposition.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form.
• продо́лжим — “(we will) continue”; perfective future, “we” form.
• путь — “path / journey”; direct object (accusative).

PRONUNCIATION
• продо́лжим: stress ДО.
• путь: final Ь softens Т.

GRAMMAR IDEA: You already know these building blocks. Russian often reuses the same useful expressions with small changes.

LYRIC 12 — EVERY PATH CIRCLES BACK TO THE START (REPRISE)
Russian: Ка́ждый путь сно́ва приво́дит к нача́лу.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Ка́ждый — “every”; masculine singular adjective.
• путь — “path”; masculine singular subject.
• сно́ва — “again”; adverb.
• приво́дит — “leads”; present-tense verb, “it” form.
• к — “toward”; preposition.
• нача́лу — “beginning”; dative noun after к.

PRONUNCIATION
• Pay attention to ка́ждый, приво́дит and нача́лу.
• Try reading the whole line without pausing between every word.

GRAMMAR IDEA: Repeated lyrics are a chance to recognize the same grammar more quickly.

LYRIC 13 — CARS ARE RUSHING DOWN THE ROAD
Russian: Маши́ны мча́тся по доро́ге.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Маши́ны — “cars”; plural noun, subject (nominative).
• мча́тся — “rush / speed along”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• по — “along”; preposition taking the dative here.
• доро́ге — “road”; dative form of доро́га after по.

PRONUNCIATION
• маши́ны: stress ШИ; Ш is hard even before И.
• мча́тся: stress ЧА; begin with МЧ, and say -ТСЯ approximately as one “tsa” sound.
• доро́ге: stress РО; the Г before Е is softened.

GRAMMAR IDEA: по доро́ге = “along the road.” Russian uses a changed ending after по.

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 14 — BUSES ARE STOPPING AND SLOWING DOWN
Russian: Авто́бусы остана́вливаются и замедля́ют ход.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Авто́бусы — “buses”; plural noun, subject (nominative).
• остана́вливаются — “(they) stop / are stopping”; present-tense reflexive verb, “they” form.
• и — “and”; conjunction joining actions.
• замедля́ют — “(they) slow”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• ход — “pace / movement”; direct object (accusative), the pace being slowed.

PRONUNCIATION
• авто́бусы: stress ТО; the first А is unstressed.
• остана́вливаются: stress НА. Break it into о-ста-НА-вли-ва-ют-ся.
• замедля́ют: stress ЛЯ; Я softens the preceding Л.
• In остана́вливаются, -ТСЯ makes a “tsa” sound at the end.

GRAMMAR IDEA: Both verbs end in forms describing what THEY do. The -ся ending belongs to a reflexive verb; it is not a separate word.

LYRIC 15 — TRAINS ARE MOVING STRAIGHT ON THE TRACKS
Russian: Поезда́ иду́т пря́мо по ре́льсам.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Поезда́ — “trains”; plural noun, subject (nominative); singular is по́езд.
• иду́т — “(they) go / are moving”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• пря́мо — “straight”; direction adverb.
• по — “along”; preposition taking the dative here.
• ре́льсам — “rails / tracks”; plural dative form of ре́льсы.

PRONUNCIATION
• поезда́: stress ДА. The singular по́езд has different stress.
• иду́т: stress ДУТ.
• ре́льсам: stress РЕ; Ь makes Л soft.

GRAMMAR IDEA: иду́т can describe trains traveling on a route, not only people walking.

LYRIC 16 — PLANES ARE FLYING IN THE SKY ABOVE
Russian: Самолёты летя́т высоко́ в не́бе.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Самолёты — “airplanes”; plural noun, subject (nominative).
• летя́т — “(they) fly / are flying”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• высоко́ — “high / up high”; adverb.
• в — “in”; preposition indicating location.
• не́бе — “sky”; prepositional form of не́бо after в.

PRONUNCIATION
• самолёты: Ё is stressed; no extra vowel needs to be added after Л.
• летя́т: stress Я; Я softens the preceding Т.
• высоко́: stress the final О.
• в не́бе: link the short В closely to the next word.

GRAMMAR IDEA: в не́бе = “in the sky”; the ending -Е signals the location form.

------------------------------------------------------------
LYRIC 17 — BOATS ARE FLOATING ON IN THE WATERS
Russian: Ло́дки плыву́т да́льше по воде́.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Ло́дки — “boats”; plural noun, subject (nominative).
• плыву́т — “(they) float / sail / move through water”; present-tense verb, “they” form.
• да́льше — “farther / onward”; adverb.
• по — “along / through”; preposition taking the dative here.
• воде́ — “water”; dative form of вода́ after по.

PRONUNCIATION
• ло́дки: stress ЛО; Д and К are joined without an inserted vowel.
• плыву́т: stress ВУ; Ы is not English “ee” — pull the tongue back slightly.
• да́льше: stress ДА; Ь softens Л.
• воде́: stress ДЕ; the first О is unstressed and reduced.

GRAMMAR IDEA: по воде́ = “along/on the water,” using the same по + dative pattern as по доро́ге.

LYRIC 18 — UP AND DOWN, UP AND DOWN, UP AND DOWN YOU GO
Russian: Вверх и вниз, вверх и вниз, вверх и вниз идёшь.

WORD-BY-WORD (the same words repeat)
• вверх — “up / upward”; direction adverb.
• и — “and”; conjunction.
• вниз — “down / downward”; direction adverb.
• идёшь — “you go / are going”; present-tense verb, informal “you” form.

PRONUNCIATION
• вверх: Х is a rough, breathy sound at the back of the mouth.
• вниз: the final З sounds like С because voiced consonants become voiceless at word endings.
• идёшь: Ё is stressed; final Ш has no additional vowel.

GRAMMAR IDEA: вверх and вниз are adverbs, not nouns. They describe the direction of movement.

LYRIC 19 — THANK YOU FOR WALKING THIS PATH WITH ME
Russian: Спаси́бо, что прошёл э́тот путь со мной.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Спаси́бо — “thank you”; fixed expression of gratitude.
• что — “that / for the fact that”; connects the thanks to the action.
• прошёл — “(you) walked / completed”; masculine singular past-tense verb.
• э́тот — “this”; masculine accusative form, agreeing with путь.
• путь — “path”; masculine noun, direct object (accusative).
• со — “with”; preposition.
• мной — “me”; instrumental form after со.

PRONUNCIATION
• спаси́бо: stress СИ; unstressed O at the end is reduced.
• что: pronounced approximately “shto,” not “chto.”
• прошёл: Ё is stressed. After Ш, the stressed Ё sounds like “o,” not “yo.”
• путь: final Ь softens Т; со мной forms one natural phrase.

GRAMMAR IDEA: прошёл addresses a male in this form. To address a female, say прошла́.

LYRIC 20 — LET'S MAKE THIS JOURNEY TOGETHER AGAIN
Russian: Дава́й пройдём э́тот путь вме́сте сно́ва.

WORD-BY-WORD
• Дава́й — “let's”; informal invitation word used before the action.
• пройдём — “(we will) go through / complete”; perfective future, “we” form.
• э́тот — “this”; masculine accusative adjective/pronoun agreeing with путь.
• путь — “path / journey”; masculine noun, direct object (accusative).
• вме́сте — “together”; adverb.
• сно́ва — “again”; adverb.

PRONUNCIATION
• дава́й: stress ВАЙ; Й is a short Y-like sound.
• пройдём: stress Ё; don't insert extra vowels between the consonants.
• вме́сте: stress МЕ; link ВМ smoothly.
• сно́ва: stress СНО.

GRAMMAR IDEA: Дава́й + пройдём = “let's go through it / complete it.” Вме́сте shows that we do it together.

SIX QUICK PATTERNS TO REMEMBER

1. VERBS CHANGE WITH THE SUBJECT
   Мы стои́м = WE stand.
   Ты идёшь = YOU go.
   Они́ бегу́т = THEY run.
   Поезда́ иду́т = TRAINS go.
   Ло́дки плыву́т = BOATS sail.

2. A NOUN'S ENDING CAN CHANGE ITS ROLE
   доро́га = road (subject / dictionary form)
   доро́гу = road (direct object)
   по доро́ге = along the road (dative)

3. AFTER к, EXPECT THE DATIVE
   к нача́лу = toward the beginning
   к концу́ = toward the end

4. AFTER до, EXPECT THE GENITIVE
   до конца́ = as far as / until the end

5. AFTER со, USE THE INSTRUMENTAL
   со мной = with me

6. WATCH THE SPELLINGS THAT CHANGE THE SOUND
   Ё = always stressed in these song lines.
   Ь = softens the preceding consonant; it is not a syllable.
   -тся = commonly pronounced “tsa.”
   Ы = a Russian vowel without an exact English equivalent.
   Unstressed О usually sounds reduced, often closer to “a/uh.”

STUDENT PRACTICE
1. Listen to a single lyric.
2. Underline the verb (action word).
3. Identify who is doing that action.
4. Circle the stressed vowel in each longer Russian word.
5. Say the lyric slowly, then at a normal pace.
6. Speak once along with the music and once without reading.
